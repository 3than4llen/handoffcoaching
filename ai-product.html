The model is the part your engineers can swap out next quarter.

The interface around it is what your users live with every day. What they see while it's working, and how they tell a good answer from a confident wrong one.

Those are design problems. On most AI teams they get solved by whoever has time that sprint, and it shows in activation and retention long before anyone calls it a design issue.

What I work on

Input and steering

Controls and presets that let people say what they mean without first learning your model's quirks.

Showing the work

Sources and confidence, shown at the level of detail your user can actually act on.

Review and approval

Human-in-the-loop flows for AI actions with real consequences, like a diagnosis note or a cart rerouted across a factory floor.

Errors and recovery

Failure states and undo for output that finds new ways to be wrong every week.

Waiting

Streaming and partial results, so a ten-second response feels like progress instead of a hang.

New mental models

Getting experts to adopt tools that work differently from the ones they trained on. At LVIS that meant bringing 3D brain-network views to physicians trained on 2D EEG traces.

Recent work

LVIS NeuroMatch

Design lead
Nov 2022 – Jan 2025

Problem

Epileptologists read EEGs as long 2D traces. LVIS built AI that decodes brain networks and shows them in 4D, in the cloud, with remote collaboration. The hard part was getting classically trained physicians to use it in a real diagnosis.

What I did

I built the UX workflows from hospital use cases and user journeys, then designed the UI for dense 4D visualizations, fast inline annotation and reporting that mirrors standard hospital workflows. I led design inside a 20+ person Scrum of Scrums team split between Korea and the US, and created the company's brand from scratch.

How do you introduce 3D analysis tools to physicians who are trained in 2D?

Amazon Robotics

Senior product designer, Canvas Technology
2016 – 2017

Problem

A new product category: self-driving carts moving through warehouses and factories, and operations managers who need to run the whole fleet from one screen.

What I did

I worked with the founding team to invent the interface. Live tracking over a map with each cart's video feed, stop and resume, rerouting, routes built from waypoints, no-go and low-speed zones, and analytics for alerts and route efficiency. We tested prototypes with employees and customers, and ended up basing navigation on a familiar TV remote.

Are flyout menus the solution?

Deployed with Walmart, GE and Siemens. Canvas was acquired by Amazon.

Palo

Design lead
2025

Problem

Palo was built by the team behind the most-viewed YouTube clip in history. The product helps creators make YouTube Shorts, and every Short can be laid out in a huge number of complex, highly visual variations. Creators needed a way to steer all of that without fighting a dense editor.

What I did

I designed the chat-based interface that lets creators shape and compare those variations by talking to the AI, until they land on the version they want to put out. I managed a team of four designers, set the brand guidelines for the product and public website, and oversaw the design system.
